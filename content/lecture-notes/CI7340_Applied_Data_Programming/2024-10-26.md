
# Statistics



Here are the key features of a random experiment:

1. **Uncertainty**: The outcome of a random experiment is uncertain and cannot be predicted with certainty.
2. **Randomness**: The outcome of a random experiment is governed by chance, rather than by any deterministic rule.
3. **Well-defined set of outcomes**: A random experiment has a well-defined set of possible outcomes, which are often called the **sample space**.
4. **Probabilities**: Each outcome in the sample space has a probability of occurring, which is a number between 0 and 1.
5. **Independence**: The outcome of a random experiment does not depend on the outcome of any previous experiments.
6. **Repeatability**: A random experiment can be repeated under the same conditions, and the probability of each outcome remains the same.
7. **No predictability**: The outcome of a random experiment cannot be predicted with certainty, even with complete knowledge of the experiment.

These features are often referred to as the **axioms of probability**, and they form the foundation of probability theory.
